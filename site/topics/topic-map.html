Topic Map

This site has six learning sections that build on each other, plus guides and a glossary you can dip into at any point. Here is how they connect and the order to take them in, depending on who you are and what you want out of this.

1. AI Concepts

The foundation everything else stands on: what LLMs actually are, how tokens and context windows work, what temperature changes, and how embeddings give models memory-like recall. Learn this first no matter your background, because every other section assumes this vocabulary.

Feeds into: AI Safety (you cannot reason about risks without knowing how the model works), Using AI Agents, and Building Agents (context window and cost thinking comes straight from here).

2. AI Safety

How to use these tools responsibly: what can go wrong, how to spot it, and the checklists that keep you out of trouble. Treat this as the license you earn before you start driving, not as fine print.

Feeds into: Using AI Agents and Coding with AI Agents (safe daily habits), and Building Agents (safety by design in systems you ship to others).

3. Using AI Agents

The daily practice: prompting well, breaking work into agent-shaped tasks, reviewing output critically, and building small workflows that save you real hours. This is where the learning starts paying off in your actual job.

Feeds into: Coding with AI Agents (same skills, applied to code) and Customizing Agents (once you know what you want, you can shape the tool to deliver it).

4. Coding with AI Agents

Pair programming with AI: writing, debugging, testing, and reviewing code with an agent at your side. You stay the engineer in charge; the agent is the tireless junior on your team.

Feeds into: Customizing Agents (tuning the assistant to your codebase) and Building Agents (the development habits you practice here scale up to full systems).

5. Customizing Agents

Shaping agents for a specific job: system prompts, tools, knowledge sources, and behavior tuning. This is the bridge between using someone else's agent and building your own.

Feeds into: Building Agents (customization patterns become system components) and the Guides on model selection and evals (you need both once your custom agent goes to real users).

6. Building Agents

The full craft: designing agent systems, wiring tools and memory, evaluating behavior, controlling costs, and deploying to production. This section pulls together everything before it.

Feeds into: the Guides (run your first eval, control costs, pick a model) which become daily practice, and the workshop guide when you are ready to teach others what you built.

Suggested learning paths

The non-technical knowledge worker

You want AI to make your workweek lighter, not a new career. Go in order through 1. AI Concepts, 2. AI Safety, and 3. Using AI Agents, then stop and practice for two weeks before touching anything else. Your goal: three real workflows from your job that an agent now handles. Come back to the Guides only if you start building something others will use.

The software engineer

You ship code and want AI to make you faster without making you sloppy. Skim 1. AI Concepts for the vocabulary, read 2. AI Safety fully (your code will touch user data), then go deep on 3. Using AI Agents, 4. Coding with AI Agents, and 5. Customizing Agents. Finish with the Guides on evals and costs. Your goal: an agent tuned to your team's codebase that passes its own eval suite.

The AI engineer

You are building AI systems or plan to. Take everything in order, 1 through 6, and do the exercises instead of just reading them. Pay special attention to 2. AI Safety and 6. Building Agents, then treat the Guides (model selection, cost control, evals) as your weekly practice loop. Your goal: a deployed agent with measured accuracy and cost per task, and the ability to run a workshop teaching someone else to do the same.
